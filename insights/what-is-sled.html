What is SLED?

SLED means state, local and education government. Here is who is in it, how they buy IT and why it matters if you sell to the public sector.

By Shahzaib Ahmad Bajwa, Founder. Published October 18, 2026

Short answer: SLED is the US public sector minus the federal government. It covers the 50 states and their agencies, more than 91,000 local governments, and the public schools, colleges and universities they fund.

What the letters stand for

S is state. All 50 state governments and their agencies: transportation, health and human services, revenue, IT and many more. Each state runs its own budget and purchasing rules.

L is local. Counties, cities, towns, townships and special districts. Special districts are single-purpose governments, such as water, fire, transit or port authorities. They are the largest group.

E is education. Public K-12 school districts and public higher education, including community colleges and state universities.

How many SLED organizations are there?

The US Census counted 91,438 local governments in 2025, before you add the 50 states, their agencies and public colleges.

That means SLED is not one market. It is tens of thousands of separate buyers, each with its own budget, board or council, and purchasing policy.

How much does SLED spend on IT?

State and local IT spending was forecast above $154 billion in 2025, up 6.4%, split roughly evenly between states and local governments. Other forecasts use different definitions, so treat any single number as an estimate.

Source: Vista Point Advisors, GovTech report, Q3 2025.

State CIOs ranked AI, cybersecurity, cost control, modernization, digital services and accessibility among their top priorities for 2026. Accessibility made the list on its own for the first time, driven by the ADA Title II web rule.

Source: NASCIO State CIO Top 10 Priorities, 2026.

How SLED organizations buy IT

  • Micro-purchases. Small buys with little or no competition. For purchases paid with federal award money, the micro-purchase threshold is $15,000 from Oct 1, 2025.
  • Informal quotes. Usually 2 or 3 written quotes. Ceilings vary widely, from $25,000 for some Texas state purchases to $250,000 for documented quotes in Colorado.
  • Formal solicitations. An invitation for bids, where the lowest responsive bid wins, or a request for proposals, where best value wins.
  • Cooperative contracts. An agency buys from a contract another public entity already competed. Texas DIR's deliverables-based IT contracts are 1 example, open to Texas state, local and education buyers.

Sources: OMB revisions to 2 CFR 200 (90 FR 41872); E&I Cooperative Services, Small Dollar Thresholds by State, Dec 2024; Texas DIR.

Timing matters too. 46 states start their fiscal year on July 1 (NCSL). Budgets are built in spring, and new-year purchase orders often open in July and August.

What this means if you sell IT to SLED

Small, well-scoped projects can move in weeks under quote thresholds. Larger work often takes 6 to 18 months without a contract vehicle. Many smaller firms start as subcontractors to primes that already hold a vehicle.
